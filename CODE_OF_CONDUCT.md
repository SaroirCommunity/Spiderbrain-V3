# Code of conduct

## Our pledge

We, the maintainers and contributors of spiderbrain, pledge to make participation in this project a respectful, harassment-free experience for everyone - regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity or expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Our standards

**Examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive environment:**

- Using welcoming and inclusive language.
- Being respectful of differing viewpoints, experiences, and skill levels.
- Gracefully accepting constructive feedback on code, design, or methodology.
- Focusing on what is best for the community and the project.
- Showing empathy toward other contributors, especially first-time contributors.
- Crediting prior work and other contributors.

**Examples of unacceptable behaviour:**

- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information (physical or electronic address, employer, real name when a pseudonym is the contributor's preference, etc.) without explicit permission.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention or advances.
- Dismissing a contribution because of who made it rather than what it does.
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Project-specific norms

Beyond the standard pledge, spiderbrain has a few norms that grow out of the project's standing claim of intellectual honesty:

- **Honesty over hype.** When discussing measurements, methodology, or claims, separate what is measured from what is modelled from what is estimated. The honesty contract in `docs/benchmarks.md` §0 binds contributions, not just the maintainer.
- **Disagreement is welcome, contempt is not.** Two contributors can reasonably disagree about whether the cascade engine should grow a feature, whether v4 is ready, or whether the spider metaphor earns its place. The maintainer makes the final call. Pressuring others by personal attack, doxxing, or sustained negativity is not the path to changing that call.
- **Negative results are first-class.** If your benchmark, screenshot, or adapter shows the brain does not help (or hurts) on your stack, that is a valuable contribution. We mean the "happy to be proved wrong" line in `CHALLENGES.md` literally; contributors who bring honest negative data will be credited the same as contributors who bring honest positive data.
- **Don't punch down on first-time contributors.** A new OSS contributor whose PR has rough edges is doing the project a favour, not the other way round. Constructive review, by example, is the standard.

## Scope

This code of conduct applies in all project spaces - the GitHub repository, the issue tracker, pull requests, discussion threads, mailing lists, and any public spaces where an individual is representing the project or its community. Examples include using an official project email address, posting via an official social media account, or acting as an appointed representative at an online or offline event.

The code also applies to private communications between project participants when those communications relate to project work.

## Enforcement

Instances of unacceptable behaviour may be reported by emailing **contact@perform.digital** with `[CONDUCT]` in the subject line. All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly. The maintainers are obligated to respect the privacy and security of the reporter of any incident.

### Enforcement actions

The maintainer will follow these guidelines when determining the consequences of any action they deem in violation of this code of conduct:

1. **Correction.** A private, written warning from the maintainer, providing clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behaviour was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.
2. **Warning.** A warning with consequences for continued behaviour. No interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the code, for a specified period of time. This includes avoiding interactions in project spaces as well as external channels like social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.
3. **Temporary ban.** A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the community for a specified period of time (typically 30 days). No public or private interaction with the people involved is allowed during this period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.
4. **Permanent ban.** A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the community.

The maintainer reserves the right to skip steps for behaviour that is severe (harassment, threats, doxxing, deliberate disclosure of security vulnerabilities outside the responsible-disclosure path).
